Lesson Plan: Lecture 01 — Course Overview & the Landscape of Advanced Algorithms

Course: CS G526 Advanced Algorithms & Complexity Duration: 50 Minutes Topic: Introduction to Advanced Algorithmic Paradigms Objectives: 1. Situate the course in the overall algorithmic toolkit (beyond core Data Structures and Algorithms). 2. Define and contrast the four core paradigms: Randomized, Approximation, Game-theoretic/Online, and Parallel/Distributed. 3. Understand the syllabus roadmap, evaluation components, and course logistics.

00:00–00:15 15 mins Welcome & Course Logistics: Introduce course info, evaluation scheme, textbooks (T1, T2, reference books), consultation hours, and lab timings. Establish expectations. Highlight the 20% weight on the Term Project (Proposal + Presentation + Report).
00:15–00:35 20 mins What is “Advanced”? The Four Paradigms: Discuss why standard polynomial-time exact sequential algorithms are insufficient. Introduce: (1) Randomized (trading certainty for efficiency); (2) Approximation (trading optimality for runtime); (3) Game-theoretic/Online (handling incomplete info); (4) Parallel & Distributed (scaling via hardware). Board Work: Write down the 4 paradigms with a one-sentence summary of what they trade off (e.g. certainty, optimality, space/time, etc.).
00:35–00:45 10 mins Application Domains: Illustrate paradigms using real-world domains like PageRank/Rec Systems (Online/Randomized) and Sequence Alignment/BLAST (Approximation/Parallel). Real-world context: Show how modern services (Google search, Amazon recommendations) are built on these paradigms rather than standard sorting/searching.
00:45–00:50 5 mins Concept Check & Next Class Preview: Run a brief concept check to test intuition on the 4 paradigms. Preview Lecture 2 (Probability Review). Active learning. Gauge student readiness for mathematical review.

❓ Concept Checks & Interactive Questions

  1. Paradigms and Uncertainty: > Question: “Which algorithmic paradigm specifically handles uncertainty in: (a) Inputs arriving dynamically over time? (b) Algorithm runtime? (c) Solution quality for NP-hard problems?” > Answer: > - (a) Online / Game-theoretic Algorithms (decisions must be made without knowing the future). > - (b) Randomized Algorithms (specifically Las Vegas algorithms where runtime is a random variable, but the answer is always correct). > - (c) Approximation Algorithms (we guarantee a solution within a factor \alpha of the optimal).

  2. The Project Proposal Requirement: > Question: “What constitutes a good Term Project topic for this course?” > Answer: An ideal project involves either applying one of the four paradigms to a domain of interest (e.g., bio-informatics, web graphs, parallel systems) or implementing and evaluating/extending a modern algorithm from recent literature.
