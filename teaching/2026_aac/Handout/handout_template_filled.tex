\section{Course Description:}
This course explores advanced paradigms in algorithm design and analysis beyond the core curriculum: randomized algorithms, approximation algorithms, game-theoretic techniques, and parallel \& distributed algorithms, with applications to modern domains such as the Internet/Web and computational biology.

\section{Scope \& Objective of the Course:}
The course equips students with the mathematical tools and algorithmic techniques needed to reason about computationally hard problems and to design efficient algorithms under uncertainty. Emphasis is placed on both rigorous theoretical analysis and practical algorithm engineering.

\section{Course learning outcomes}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\bfseries{ CLO{\arabic*}.}, 
                leftmargin=2cm]
 \item Design and analyze randomized (Las Vegas \& Monte Carlo) algorithms and reason about their success probability using tail bounds.
 \item Apply polynomial identity testing, the Schwartz--Zippel lemma, and the Tutte-matrix framework to randomized algorithmic problems.
 \item Design and analyze approximation algorithms using greedy, LP-rounding, primal-dual, and local-search techniques, and understand limits of approximation.
 \item Model strategic and online settings using game-theoretic and online-algorithmic tools, and analyze them via competitive ratios and equilibria.
 \item Describe the key paradigms of parallel/distributed algorithms and algorithms for the Web/Internet and computational biology.
 \item Survey and present current literature on an advanced topic in algorithms, culminating in a term project.
\end{enumerate}
